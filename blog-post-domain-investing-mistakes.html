7 Domain Investing Mistakes I Made (and What They Cost Me)

When I started buying domains, I thought it was easy: buy a name, wait a little, sell it for more. It wasn’t like that for me. I made a lot of mistakes, and I paid for every one of them. Here are seven, and what I do differently now.

Short answer: Domain investing can be profitable, but not by luck. The mistakes that cost me the most were ignoring renewal prices, buying names I liked, not checking expired domains, skipping the trademark check, buying too many names, guessing prices and not keeping records. Check the numbers first, and be willing to wait.

If you’re asking whether domain investing is profitable, I hope this gives you a more honest answer than most videos do. Plenty of people make it sound easy. It isn’t, but you can avoid the mistakes that hurt me.

1. I only looked at the first-year price

When I bought my first names, I was sure they would sell right away. That was the plan in my head.

Then the renewal bill came, and it was double. I had assumed I would pay the same price I paid when I bought. I was wrong.

That bill changed how I buy. I stopped buying just because a name looked good. I learned to check the potential of a name and to look at different numbers about it. Now I ask myself two things: would I buy this name myself, and why? You can see real renewal prices side by side in my domain renewal price comparison.

2. I bought names because I liked them

Almost every name I bought early on felt like it would sell fast. I really believed that. A year later I looked at my account and saw no leads. Nobody had even asked about them.

I didn’t figure out my mistake alone. I started spending time in domain forums, and I read a lot. Slowly I saw where I was going wrong: I was buying with my heart and not thinking about who the buyer would be.

The ending of the domain matters too. In my CircleID article on the new extension fight, I wrote that only about 31% of new extensions get renewed, while .com is around 75%. A lot of people buy the new ones. Most don’t keep them.

3. I bought expired domains without checking them

I thought an expired domain was a golden egg: cheap, with history, and sure to sell. This is one of the most common mistakes when buying expired domains, and I made it.

Then I looked closer. The name had spam backlinks. Some came from adult sites, and there were redirects from betting sites. There were other problems too. I couldn’t use it or sell it.

Now I never bid before checking. I start with the Wayback Machine to see what the site used to be, then I go through every metric I can find.

4. I didn’t think about trademarks

People ask me, is domain flipping illegal? No. Buying and selling domains is legal. But a name that contains someone’s trademark is a different story.

I found that out when I got an email from a company. The name was their trademark. I had no choice but to come to an agreement with them. It was a bad experience, and I felt really let down. Maybe I’ll write about it in full one day.

Now I check everything before I decide. If you want to do the same, here is my guide on how to check a domain for trademark problems.

5. I bought too many names

In the beginning, every name I looked at seemed good. It was like first love, a teenager’s love. I’ll admit that with a laugh now.

I didn’t notice the problem until much later. Everything needs a limit, and I had none. Every name you buy has a yearly cost, so the more you buy, the more it adds up. Nobody stopped me. I stopped when I understood it myself.

If you don’t know how much to set aside, I wrote about what domain investing really costs per year.

6. I had no plan for pricing

I’ve been on both sides of this. I once kept a name at a high price and got no offers. When I lowered it, I managed to sell it. Another time I sold a name too cheap and felt sorry about it later.

That’s part of this business, and part of life too. People’s wants never end, and you can’t read every buyer’s mind. What I do now is take the middle road. I don’t go too high and I don’t go too low.

If you want a place to start, you can use my free domain appraisal tool. It gives a score and a rough value for any name. It’s only a guide. In the end, a domain sells for what one real buyer is ready to pay.

7. I didn’t keep a record

At the start, I didn’t write things down. I forgot when names expired, what I paid, and where I bought them.

The worst part was renewal time. Sometimes there was no money on my card, and then came all kinds of trouble.

Now I keep a spreadsheet, and I pin the important dates on a board where I can’t miss them. Nothing gets away from me anymore.

What I learned from all this

When you start working, you learn every part of it with your own hands. One mistake can teach you something you won’t find in any book. Time and life teach you things that are worth more than anything you read.

If you stop working because you’re afraid of mistakes, the learning stops too. So make your mistakes, and take a lesson from each one.

My biggest lessons are simple. Be patient. Keep going, day after day. And keep yourself up to date with the trends. There’s no shortcut for that. If I could tell a beginner just one thing, it would be this: be patient, and learn a little every day. Watch what is happening in the market.

One book I’d read if I were starting today

If you want to keep learning, read The Psychology of Money by Morgan Housel. It’s not about domains. But it teaches what matters most here: patience and self-control. The big idea is that being smart or finding a good deal isn’t enough. You also need patience, self-control and long-term thinking.

  • Be patient. Don’t buy a good name and expect a quick sale. The right buyer can take a long time to show up.
  • Stay disciplined. Don’t buy because you’re excited. Don’t sell too cheap because you’re nervous.

If I had to pick one lesson, it’s this. Buying a good domain makes you an investor. Being able to wait for the right time to sell is what can make you a successful one.

So, is domain investing profitable?

It can be, but not by luck. The people who make money are the ones who check the numbers first, and who are willing to wait.

Before you buy any name, ask yourself:

  • What will it cost to renew?
  • Who would buy it?
  • Is the history clean?
  • Is there a trademark problem?
  • Can I afford it this year?
  • What price will I ask?

If you can answer those six, you’re already ahead of most beginners.

Frequently asked questions

Is domain investing profitable?

It can be, but not for everyone. You make money by choosing names that real buyers want and by keeping your yearly costs low. Many names never sell, so plan for that.

Is domain flipping illegal?

No. Buying and selling domains is legal. What isn’t allowed is buying a name with someone’s trademark in it just to sell it back to them.

What are the most common domain flipping mistakes for beginners?

Ignoring renewal costs, buying names you like instead of names buyers need, buying too many names, and guessing the price.

What are the mistakes when buying expired domains?

Not checking the history. Look at what the site used to be, which sites link to it, and whether it was ever used for spam.

How long does it take to sell a domain?

There’s no fixed time. Some sell in a few weeks. Many take years, and some never sell. That’s why patience and low yearly costs matter.

How much money do I need to start?

You can start small. Think about how much you can afford to lose, then set a yearly budget and keep to it.

Should I buy many domains or just a few?

Start with a few. They’re easier to track and the bills stay small. Add more after you’ve made a sale and know what works.

What should I do with a domain nobody asks about?

After a year with no interest, lower the price or let it expire. Don’t keep paying for a name you’ve stopped believing in.
