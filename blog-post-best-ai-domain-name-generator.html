Best AI Domain Name Generators, Tested With Real Availability Checks

I typed one idea, "legal tech startup", into Namelix, GoDaddy and Instant Domain Search, then re-checked the names at Namecheap. The tools often disagreed with each other, and the price on the results page was not always the price I would pay.

Short answer: Namelix gave me the best ideas, Instant Domain Search gave me the fastest read on what was taken, and GoDaddy was the easiest to read on price. None of them was enough alone. One name showed as taken in one tool and was for sale in two others, and one premium name renewed at four times its first-year price. Use the tools to find and shortlist names, then confirm at a registrar's checkout.

By Imteaz (Galib) Chowdhury · Published November 21, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026

Most lists of the best AI domain name generator repeat what each tool says about itself. I wanted to see what happens when you give them all the same idea and then check the same names somewhere else.

A generator is easy to judge by how many names it gives you. The harder question is whether those names can really be registered, and for how much. That is where I found the surprises.

This is a small personal test, not a lab study. I used one idea and wrote down what was on my screen on November 21, 2026. Prices and results change, so treat every number here as a snapshot.

How I ran the test

I used one made-up search: "legal tech startup". I put it into Namelix, GoDaddy's domain search and Instant Domain Search. In GoDaddy and Instant Domain Search I also tried the shorter "legal tech". Then I searched some of the interesting names again at Namecheap, as a second opinion.

For each name I noted three things: whether it showed as available, taken, premium or aftermarket; the price on the results page; and, for one name, the price in the cart.

Namelix: good for ideas, not proof

Namelix sorted its results into themed tabs, such as "Legal Tech Innovations" and "Modern Legal Technology Terms". The first screen included TechXpress, Thinklytics, CyberThink, Combytics, Innovix, Bitopia, Biobotix and Oliofy.

Honestly, most of them sounded like general tech companies, not legal ones. Only clerkdb hinted at legal work to me. Some cards had a green check mark, and clerkdb carried a $1,295 price tag. I took the check to mean the domain looked free, but I didn't trust that without a second look.

So I tried techexpress at Namecheap, a spelling close to the TechXpress card. The .io, .org and .xyz versions were all premium, listed at $8,999, $2,988 and $1,450. A good-sounding name can still be priced in the thousands in the extension you want. Namelix gave me directions to explore, and that was its real job.

GoDaddy: easy to read prices, but read the small print

For "legal tech startup", GoDaddy mostly showed my phrase with cheap extensions, such as legaltechstartup.site at $0.99 for the first year. The crossed-out price beside it, $54.99, was the much higher list price.

One row needed a closer look. The .io version was $0.01 for the first year, but the small print said "1st yr only with 3 yr term". At Namecheap, the same .io name showed $29.98 a year. The cheapest-looking row was not the same deal.

I didn't see a .com in the rows I captured, and many rows were marked "promoted". For the shorter "legal tech", GoDaddy listed legaltech.tech as premium at $94.99 for the first year.

GoDaddy says its domain name generator won't suggest names that can't be registered. In the rows I captured, every name had a price or a buy button, which matched that.

Instant Domain Search: the fastest view of what's taken

Instant Domain Search shows the .com first, then a grid of extensions with colored status dots for available, premium, aftermarket and taken. For "legal tech", legaltech.com was taken. The counters at the top read 25 available, 3 premium, 10 aftermarket and 35 taken.

Aftermarket rows showed the asking price at once: legaltech.xyz at $98,795, legaltech.co at $29,888, legaltech.app at $9,488 and legaltech.online at $1,450. For the longer "legal tech startup", legaltechstartup.com was taken, but 71 other extensions showed as available, with none premium or aftermarket.

My read is that the short keyword name was heavily taken or priced like an asset, while the long phrase was open almost everywhere. Cheap to register is not the same as strong as a brand.

One result bothered me. Instant Domain Search showed legaltech.tech as taken. That is where the cross-check began.

The legaltech.tech example

Instant Domain Search said taken. GoDaddy and Namecheap both listed it as a premium name. At $81.25 it looked like a bargain, until I added it to the cart.

The one-year price was $81.25. The renewal price was $325.00 a year. The search results page never showed that second number. I only saw it because I opened the cart.

What the test taught me

  • Available does not mean cheap. Short keyword names were taken or priced in the thousands, while long phrases were open for under a dollar.
  • The first-year price is one of three numbers. The other two are the conditions, such as a three-year term, and the renewal price.
  • Different premium names renew differently. techexpress.org renewed at $18.98 a year after a $2,988 purchase, while legaltech.tech renewed at $325.00.
  • When tools disagree, check again. A mismatch on one name told me more than ten matches.
  • None of them checked the brand. No tool told me about trademarks, old history or what a word means in another language.

Which tool should you use?

  • For ideas: Namelix. It gave me directions I wouldn't have found alone, but the names still need checking.
  • For a quick view of many extensions: Instant Domain Search. The color-coded status and visible aftermarket prices saved time.
  • For easy-to-read prices: GoDaddy, as long as you read the term and promotion details.
  • For the final answer: the registrar's cart. I used Namecheap, but the habit matters more than the brand.

Other generators exist, but I didn't test them, so I won't rank them. If I were naming a real project, I'd work in this order:

  1. Generate ideas in one tool.
  2. Check the shortlist in a second tool.
  3. Open a registrar cart and read the renewal price.
  4. Check trademarks, history and meaning.
  5. Say the name out loud to someone.

Quick answers

What is the best AI domain name generator?

In my test, Namelix was best for ideas, Instant Domain Search was fastest for checking many extensions, and GoDaddy showed prices most clearly. None was enough on its own, so I confirmed every name at a registrar before trusting it.

Is there a good AI domain name generator that is free to use?

Namelix, GoDaddy and Instant Domain Search all let me search names and see results without buying anything. Access and limits change, so check each tool's current terms. The real cost comes when you register or renew a domain.

Is an instant domain name generator result final?

No. In my test, Instant Domain Search showed legaltech.tech as taken, while GoDaddy and Namecheap both listed it as a premium name. Treat the first result as a signal and confirm it at a registrar's checkout.

Does an available domain mean I can use the name?

No. Available only means the domain can be registered. It says nothing about trademarks, a business already using the name, or what the word means in other languages. Check those before you commit.

Why did the same name have different prices?

Registrars set their own promotions. GoDaddy showed $0.01 for the first year of one .io name, but only with a three-year term, while Namecheap showed $29.98 a year for the same name. Premium names can also renew at a much higher price than the first year.

Can ChatGPT replace a domain name generator?

It can suggest ideas, but it can't confirm what is registered today. Paste its ideas into a search tool and check them at a registrar.
