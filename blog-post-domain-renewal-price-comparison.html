Domain Renewal Prices Compared: What Registrars Really Charge in Year Two

You find a .com for $0.99, or even $0.01, and you buy it. Twelve months later the renewal email says $20 or more. The registrar did not hide anything. The first-year price was a sale price, and the renewal price was the normal one.

Short answer: Cloudflare has the cheapest .com renewal: about $11.17 a year, the same in year one and every year after. Dynadot ($11.62) and Porkbun ($11.81) are close behind and also keep the price the same every year. GoDaddy (about $22.99) and Namecheap (about $19.98, plus a small ICANN fee) charge almost double at renewal.

I made this mistake myself. When I started with domains, I understood very little. I found a short name, bought it as a .io, and never thought about what would happen the next year. When the second-year renewal bill arrived, I was shocked. Beginners almost never think about renewal at the start, and I was no different. That bill taught me more than any guide could.

This domain renewal price comparison shows what registrars charge from year two onward. On 1 November 2026 the company that runs .com raised its price, so every registrar’s cost went up, and older guides are now out of date. Below you will find the current .com prices, the three ways registrars set prices, the domain registrar hidden fees that are easy to miss, and a simple way to find the cheapest domain renewal registrar for you.

Why a .com Costs More in Year Two

Registrars use three pricing models. Once you know which one you are buying, the price jump makes sense.

At cost. Cloudflare charges what the .com registry charges, plus the ICANN fee, and adds nothing on top. When the registry raised its price on 1 November, Cloudflare’s price went up by the same amount. The registry fee moved from $10.26 to $10.97, so the lowest possible price at any registrar became $11.17.

Flat. Porkbun, Dynadot and Hover charge the same price to register, renew and transfer. They add a small markup, but the price never jumps later.

Promo, then standard. Namecheap, GoDaddy, Squarespace, IONOS and Hostinger cut the first-year price and earn the money back at renewal. Hostinger is the extreme case. A .com costs $0.01 for the first year, but only if you buy three years, and then $19.99 a year after that. Squarespace goes from $10 to $22.

There is nothing illegal about this, and it is very common. The trouble starts when you compare first-year prices and expect the second year to look the same.

Cloudflare Domain Renewal Price: Cheapest, but With Limits

The Cloudflare domain renewal price is the lowest of the big names: about $11.17 for a .com. WHOIS privacy is free and there are no extra offers at checkout. A transfer in costs the same as one renewal, and the year is added to your term.

There are limits. A domain registered at Cloudflare must use Cloudflare’s nameservers, so it does not work if you want your DNS somewhere else. Cloudflare also sells fewer extensions than the big registrars, and its support is limited. Help comes mostly through the dashboard and the community forum, not by phone. If you already use Cloudflare for DNS or a CDN, it is an easy choice. If you want a friendlier site and a real person to talk to, Porkbun costs about 64 cents more per year.

GoDaddy Domain Renewal Price: $22.99 and the Upsells

The domain renewal price at GoDaddy is $22.99 a year for a .com, plus the $0.20 ICANN fee. That is about double the registrars that charge the same price every year. Its first-year deals are very low, which is why the second bill comes as a shock.

Sources disagree on privacy. Older reviews say GoDaddy charges extra for WHOIS privacy. Newer comparisons say basic privacy is included on eligible domains. GoDaddy also sells paid “Domain Protection” plans on top of that. Do not guess. Add the domain to your cart, look at the total, and remove anything you did not ask for.

Namecheap and Other “Cheap First Year” Registrars

Namecheap’s official price list shows that from 1 November 2026 a .com costs $15.98 to register or transfer and $19.98 to renew, both before the ICANN fee. Sales can lower the first year, but if you plan to keep the domain, the renewal price is the one that counts. Free lifetime WHOIS privacy and an easy site make it popular, but it is no longer cheap at renewal.

IONOS ($10 then $20) and Squarespace ($10 then $22) follow the same pattern. Squarespace has one more catch. A transfer in costs a full year at the regular $22 renewal price, not the first-year price.

Domain Registrar Hidden Fees to Check Before You Pay

The renewal price is the biggest cost, but not the only one. Check these in your cart:

  • WHOIS privacy. Most big registrars include it now, but not every plan or extension does. Make sure it is free for the whole time you own the domain, not just year one.
  • The ICANN fee. It is only $0.20 a year, but it is why a $22.99 price becomes $23.19 at GoDaddy.
  • Add-ons switched on by default. Paid protection plans, email, website builders and extra DNS are often offered at checkout, and sometimes at auto-renewal too. Look at the cart every time.
  • Hosting-zone charges. Amazon Route 53 charges a monthly fee for the DNS zone it creates with each domain, and AWS credits cannot pay for a new registration.
  • Transfer costs. A transfer usually costs one year of renewal at the new registrar, and it adds a year to your term. ICANN rules also lock a domain for 60 days after you register it, so you cannot leave a bad price right away. The official transfer rules are on ICANN’s domain transfer page.
  • Tax and currency. Some registrars show prices before VAT or sales tax. If you pay from outside the US, your bank may also charge a currency conversion fee.

How Much Does a Domain Name Cost Per Year?

A standard .com now costs about $11 to $23 a year, depending on the registrar. Other extensions vary more. At Cloudflare in October 2026, a .net was $11.86, an .org $11.20, a .dev $12.20, a .io $50 and a .ai $80. .io and .ai cost much more at every registrar. A .ai transfer is billed as two years, because that registry renews in two-year blocks.

One warning about the .com prices. The registry’s contract allows more price increases in the coming years. Only the November 2026 increase has been announced so far, but Porkbun tells customers to expect rises of about 7% a year, so do not count on today’s price staying the same. If you are choosing between .com and newer extensions, you can read about the fight over new extensions and what it means for long-term demand.

How to Find the Cheapest Domain Renewal Registrar for You

The cheapest domain registrar renewal price is not always the best one for you. Follow these steps in order:

  1. Price the renewal, not the first year. Look for the line marked “renewal”, or add the domain to a cart set to two years.
  2. Match the registrar to your DNS. If you will use Cloudflare for DNS and CDN anyway, Cloudflare is a natural fit. If you want to choose your own nameservers, look at Porkbun or Dynadot.
  3. Keep registration separate from hosting. If you change hosting later, your domain stays in your hands and you do not need a transfer.
  4. Consider renewing for several years. Most registrars let you renew for up to 10 years at a time, which locks today’s price for those years. Only do this with a registrar you trust and a name you are sure about. If the name is new to you, check it for trademark conflicts before you commit for years.
  5. Move if you’re overpaying. A transfer costs about one year of renewal at the new registrar, but it adds a year to your term, so you rarely lose money. Check the 60-day lock first.
  6. Set a reminder instead of relying on luck. Put the renewal date in your calendar a month ahead. That gives you time to compare prices before auto-renew charges your card.

My own habit is simple. Every Black Friday I work out who is offering which discount, and I compare it with the renewal price before I decide my next step. A big discount only helps in year one. The renewal price is what you keep paying.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will .com renewal prices go up again?

Possibly. The registry’s contract allows more increases, but only the 1 November 2026 one has been announced so far. Registrars that sell at or near cost pass the increase straight to you. Others may raise their prices by more.

Is WHOIS privacy an extra charge?

It is included at Cloudflare, Porkbun, Namecheap and Dynadot. Sources describe GoDaddy differently, so check the cart. At any registrar, confirm that privacy still applies at renewal, not just in year one.

Does transferring a domain save money?

Usually, if your current registrar renews at $20 or more. You pay one renewal at the new registrar, and a year is added to your term. The 60-day lock applies after a new registration or a previous transfer, so you cannot move a brand-new domain right away.

Should I turn off auto-renew to avoid a surprise charge?

It is safer to keep auto-renew on and set a calendar reminder to check the price a month ahead. People who turn it off with no plan are the ones who lose domains they wanted to keep.

Is the cheapest registrar always the best choice?

No. Cloudflare’s price is hard to beat, but it requires Cloudflare’s nameservers. Porkbun costs a little more, but it has a friendlier site and human support. The best choice is the lowest renewal price among the registrars that suit how you run your site.

Final thoughts

Do not judge a registrar by its first-year price. Look at what you will pay from year two. For a standard .com in November 2026, Cloudflare ($11.17), Dynadot ($11.62) and Porkbun ($11.81) keep the price the same every year. GoDaddy, Namecheap, Squarespace and IONOS roughly double it after year one.

Before you buy or renew, check three things: the renewal price, what is switched on at checkout, and what it costs to leave. If you own a domain at a registrar that raises the price after year one, compare its renewal with the registrars that keep the price flat. Over four years the difference is about $48 per domain, and it takes only a few minutes to fix.
