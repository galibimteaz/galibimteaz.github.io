            Selling playbook

            Listing a domain is only the first step. Most of the work that actually produces a sale happens outside the marketplace.

            Likely buyers for this name

              Core steps

              • Clear the risks first. Run a trademark check. A name that clashes with a brand is hard to sell and can be a legal problem.
              • Set a floor and an asking price. Decide the lowest price you will accept and a higher opening ask. Do not publish a price you would be unhappy to receive.
              • Build a one-page “for sale” landing page on the domain itself: the name, a short pitch, a price or “make an offer” form, and a contact email.
              • List it in two or three marketplaces that suit its type, and point each listing to your landing page.
              • Contact buyers directly. Make a list of 20 to 50 likely end users and send a short, personal email. Outreach finds buyers who were never searching marketplaces.
              • Show the name in use. A simple mock logo, a social handle and a short brand story make the name easier to imagine.
              • Use an escrow service for payment and transfer so neither side carries the risk.
              • Be patient and track results. Many good names take months or longer to sell. Keep a log of who you contacted and when.

              How a ghostwriter helps sell a domain

              Buyers rarely pay for a name they cannot picture. Good writing turns a string of letters into a business idea. A ghostwriter can produce:

              • Listing title and description that name the buyer, the benefit and the price logic.
              • “For sale” landing page copy with a short brand story and a clear call to action.
              • Outreach email sequence (first email, follow-up, last reminder) written in your voice.
              • Brand pitch with tagline ideas, use cases and a sample “about us” paragraph.
              • Negotiation replies for common situations: low offers, “is the price firm?”, “can you hold it for me?”.
              • Posts and articles (LinkedIn, blog) that attract attention to the name over time.
